## 📸 Photo guidance to forward to the client

**Q: Should the venue photos be shot empty?**
**A: No — an empty venue photographs as lifeless.** But a crowded one looks
chaotic. The best hospitality photos are *styled, fully lit, and lightly
populated*:

**Do**
- Turn **every light on** (including signage) and shoot in the evening or at
  golden hour for warmth
- **Set the tables** — clean cutlery, glasses, a small centrepiece
- Include **1–3 people** — staff in uniform or a couple of guests, looking
  natural (chatting, being served). This is what makes a photo feel alive.
- Remove clutter — bins, cables, staff bags, dirty dishes, stacked chairs
- Straighten chairs, tables and menus
- Shoot a **mix**: wide (whole space) → medium (a seating area, the counter,
  the kitchen) → close-up (food, plating, textures, logo/signage)
- Food: shoot **fresh**, near a window if possible, plated properly
- **Landscape orientation**, highest resolution, hold the camera level

**Don't**
- Don't shoot at midday with harsh light or use the on-camera flash
- Don't use heavy filters or beauty modes
- Don't send screenshots or WhatsApp-compressed copies
- Don't photograph identifiable guests without asking — or shoot them from
  behind / slightly out of focus

*Tip: a 2-hour session per venue, with staff standing in as "guests" during a
quiet period, produces everything needed.*
